Overview

Designed to feel calm, built to earn your confidence before you arrive.

Easy Does It is a café in San Diego built on the idea of "slowing down". It provides a space for people to catch up with others, get some work done, and enjoy high-quality drinks and pastries. However, this message was scattered across Yelp, Instagram, and a website that didn't reflect this idea at all.

This project was about closing that gap by designing a website that earns the same trust the café earns in person.

The goal was to take someone from "I saw this on Instagram" all the way to "I know exactly what to expect when I walk in," without a single moment of friction along the way.

The Problem

Three problems working against the same café.

Customers often discover the café either through word of mouth or via social media. Once interest is established, the user's next step is to learn more about the café before deciding whether they want to visit. These details are scattered across Instagram, Google, and Yelp, and because Easy Does It isn't like a traditional café, some customers end up confused or uncertain.

Problem 01 Fragmented information

Key details like parking, seating, wifi, the menu, and upcoming events were scattered across Yelp, Google, social posts, and a site that didn't hold any of it together.

Problem 02 Low visit confidence

People couldn't easily answer the two questions that matter: "Is this my kind of place?" and "Can I actually plan around going here?"

Problem 03 Brand mismatch

Easy Does It was built on the idea of being intentional and unhurried. However, because of the lack of information online about its menu and limited-time offerings, customers can end up confused or having to search through their socials, contradicting the café's philosophy.

All three problems fed into each other: fragmented information created uncertainty, and that uncertainty made the brand promise feel hollow before anyone even walked through the door.

Concept

The site should work the same way the café does.

Most café websites put all their information at the forefront. But due to the philosophy of Easy Does It, we decided that we'd take a different approach, one that would encourage users to spend more time on the site and exploring the message.

Design question

How do we build a website that embodies "easy does it", calm and grounded, while still answering every practical question a visitor has?

The answer came from treating the café's motto as a design constraint. If something would feel rushed or pushy in the space, it has no business being on the site.

01
Embodied motto The site itself should feel calm, grounded, and never competing for attention.
02
Measured reveal Information unfolds at a deliberate pace, aligned with how someone actually decides to visit, not how quickly we want them to convert.
03
Guided assurance Every design decision runs through one filter: would this feel rushed or pushy? Anything that would gets slowed down, so visitors feel quietly guided rather than nudged toward a decision.
User Research

Social media is where people find the café, but the website is where they decide whether to actually go.

We talked to three people in their early 20s about how they discover cafés and what they actually need from a website. From their interviews we found that discovery happens on TikTok or Instagram (sometimes through a friend) and that they visit the website if they are considering a visit and there are details they'd like to find.

By the time someone lands on the site, they're not discovering the café anymore. They're deciding whether to trust it, and the site has only a few seconds to answer two questions: "Is this my vibe?" and "Can I actually go right now?"

Other Key Findings

01
Mobile is the only device that matters All three participants checked café sites on their phones, and broken layouts or hard-to-read text caused immediate drop-off with no willingness to give the site a second look.
03
Logistics are non-negotiable Hours, location, menu, and prices are the bare minimum, and if any of them are missing or buried, people leave. Allergen information also came up as a genuine blocker for at least one participant.
04
Visual quality signals credibility Good photography and a clean layout read as professional and trustworthy. Casual phone snapshots or low-contrast text read as "maybe this place isn't that serious."
05
Cafés are evaluated as experiences Users aren't just checking facts, they're reading atmosphere, and they expect the site's tone and visuals to match what it actually feels like to walk into the space.

Pain points

The research surfaced two underlying issues that shaped the direction of the entire project.

  • Pain Point 01 Easy Does It has a clear vibe on Instagram, but there's no consistent design system translating that into a website. The typography, colors, and layout don't carry the same intentionality as the space itself, and for a café whose whole identity is built on atmosphere, that inconsistency quietly chips away at credibility before a visitor even walks in.
  • Pain Point 02 Someone who finds Easy Does It through a well-shot reel and lands on the existing site gets whiplash. The visual gap between the social presence and the website makes the café feel less considered than it actually is, which is a problem when first impressions are being formed entirely online.
Key insight

The website is for establishing trust.

Users rarely discover cafés through websites, but instead they use them to judge legitimacy and find answers to questions they may have.

Design implication

The site doesn't need to rush users, but it does need to be clear.

Practical questions should be easy to find so visitors can slow down and actually enjoy the experience of exploring the site.
